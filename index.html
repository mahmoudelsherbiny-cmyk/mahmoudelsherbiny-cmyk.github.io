A faster close and reporting cycle

Bank reconciliations were delayed, with no clear close schedule, task ownership or completion tracking.

Actions, results and measurement

My role

I designed a close calendar and checklist with task owners, deadlines and completion status, then supervised delivery. The team performed the entries and reconciliations.

Outcome

Across three consecutive monthly closes, the cycle fell from 10 to 5 and then 3 working days: two for entries and reconciliations, with the third for the trial balance, reporting and submission to the Finance Director for review.

Measurement context

The process remains in use, with exceptional cycles taking up to 5 days, for example when a bank statement arrives late. The metric ends at report submission for review, rather than final approval.

Manufacturing business9

Product costs and connected reporting · delivered in 9 months

The legacy setup lacked a clear manufacturing and product-costing cycle. Independent Excel recalculations exposed inventory valuation and cost-of-sales discrepancies.

Actions, results and measurement

My role

I led requirements, partner negotiation, financial configuration, multi-warehouse and manufacturing setup, data preparation and opening balances. The partner supported specialist technical work while I owned the finance implementation.

Outcome

Purchasing, inventory, manufacturing, sales and accounting were connected within 9 months. A full year of transactions was recreated in parallel, reaching same-day entry across both systems.

Measurement context

Transaction records were matched; costing results changed as the costing system was introduced. Product costs, production reporting and contribution margins became available. The original discrepancy was not conclusively attributed to software or configuration.

Manufacturing case−70%

From sales mix to product decisions

Price-and-volume analysis of a sales variance led to a product-mix review. Product-level costing revealed a low-profitability item carrying excessive scrap cost.

Actions, results and measurement

My role

I established product-level costing, traced scrap costs and recommended temporarily suspending the affected item until its cost structure was addressed. I recommended a mold redesign and followed implementation; technical specialists handled design, manufacture and installation.

Decision & authority

The temporary production stop followed my analysis and direct recommendation. A second product in the mix was repriced. The new-mold purchase required owner review and approval.

Outcome

The item returned to production after the mold was changed. Scrap per operating hour fell 70% with broadly comparable product and operating conditions. Mold delivery and installation took approximately 3 months.

Measurement context

Product weight also changed, so the total unit-cost difference cannot be attributed solely to scrap reduction. No quantified profitability improvement is claimed for the separate price increase. Labor and expense savings are not presented as verified efficiency percentages.

Construction business25

Anticipating a cash shortfall

The monthly plan did not capture the first quarterly installment of a new loan alongside tax and payroll commitments, creating a prospective cash shortfall.

Actions, results and measurement

My role

I rebuilt a rolling 45–90-day cash budget, assessed alternatives and negotiated a 2% discount with a customer for settlement within 10 days, within my delegated authority.

Outcome

The collection covered the shortfall, with funds in the account 25 days before the cash requirement.

Measurement context

The outcome reflects this case and its available alternatives. The discount is not an annualized financing rate or a claim of being the cheapest option in every circumstance.

From overdue receivables to settlement

A customer had significantly overdue receivables. In a management discussion, I proposed a time-bound settlement to turn an aging balance into cash.

Decision, negotiation and outcome

My role & authority

I reviewed the overdue balance, proposed a two-day settlement offer with a 2% discount within my delegated authority, and negotiated directly with the customer.

Outcome

The customer paid on the day of negotiation; the funds appeared in the bank account the following day.

Case context

This case focuses on overdue receivables, settlement terms and bank timing. No customer name, balance or recovery-rate claim is published. The 2% is a settlement discount, not an annualized financing rate.
